A policy-and-technical-level session (~38 minutes, heavily degraded audio) on the evidence base and systemic challenges behind adolescent nutrition programming in Bangladesh. Key points: an economic argument for investing in fortification (cost figures cited but not fully recoverable from the audio); national survey data showing 42% anaemia prevalence among children aged 6-59 months, plus adolescent-specific anaemia figures by severity; a call for a genuinely joint, cross-ministry "National Strategy" rather than one owned solely by the Education Department; and recurring emphasis on supply-chain reliability and monitoring/evaluation coordination between agencies (including IPHN). Practical guidance covered household-level food-preparation tips to preserve iron content, pairing supplementation with a protein-rich diet, and a strong link drawn between deworming (helminthiasis control, ~80-90% relevance) and anaemia reduction. The session closes with district-level field learnings and an appeal for more reliable supply chains and demand generation.
