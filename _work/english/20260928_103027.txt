The speaker opens by thanking participants who travelled in from different districts to attend, and thanks the organisers for the arrangements.

Anaemia data among adolescents: citing the National Micronutrient Survey, the speaker states that 28.9% of adolescents are affected by anaemia. Looking at specific sub-groups, the data shows an even higher burden -- with one figure cited at 53.1% for a particular group discussed in the session. The speaker acknowledges that although a great deal of work has already been done, the anaemia burden remains high, which is precisely why continued, targeted work at the adolescent stage matters.

Framing adolescence as a "second window of opportunity": the speaker describes adolescence as a critical second opportunity in the life cycle to correct nutritional deficits, alongside the well-established first window during early childhood. Bangladesh, the speaker notes, does not yet have a dedicated national strategy specifically for adolescent nutrition, though two relevant operational guidelines currently exist -- one general operational guideline, and a separate one addressing out-of-school adolescents.

In-school programme design: the school-based programme runs nutrition-education sessions for both boys and girls, includes height/weight and BMI measurement with referral for follow-up care, and incorporates guidance on physical activity alongside dietary advice.

Programme delivery and district-level implementation: Nutrition International has run the biscuit-based school nutrition programme since 2022, building on earlier preparatory work, and provides technical and coordination support to participating districts. Implementation includes fortified-biscuit distribution, teacher training/orientation, and support to keep "adolescent clubs" (kishor-kishori clubs) functioning in schools, with monthly coordination meetings at the district level and a district-level review roughly twice a year involving the Civil Surgeon's office. Schools report data upward through the sub-district (upazila) education office to the district education office, and the programme uses a tracking/reporting system (referred to in the recording as a "tracker") to monitor coverage; one guideline referenced is that every school should have a functioning adolescent club, with a target of roughly 80% of schools reporting each cycle. Coverage figures for iron-folic-acid tablet distribution were cited as around 71% in one period, falling to about 61% by 2020, which the speaker links to supply interruptions -- specifically a shortage of iron-folic-acid tablets reported around February-March of the relevant year in a number of districts. A poster-based communication campaign was also produced to convey nutrition messages directly to adolescents.

Coordination with other partners: the programme coordinates with UNICEF in areas where both organisations are active, aiming to ensure school coverage is not duplicated and that districts are divided sensibly between partners.

Q&A / discussion segment: A Deputy Civil Surgeon from Jamalpur asked about the relationship between the salt-iodisation intervention and iron-folic-acid supplementation for the adolescent group, and about high rates of Vitamin D deficiency being observed (a related study is referenced, said to have sampled around 5,000 participants across age groups nationally, with a headline finding that roughly 90% of participants were Vitamin D deficient; the report from this study is in the process of being disseminated). Another exchange concerned rice fortification: it was noted that fortification is currently mandated only for rice distributed through specific government safety-net/food-friendly channels, and that discussions are ongoing with government about whether to make rice fortification mandatory more broadly, alongside parallel discussion of wheat/flour fortification and Vitamin A.

Closing: the speaker lists the programme's main outstanding challenges as (1) scaling up the programme's geographic/institutional coverage and (2) strengthening the supply chain and inter-ministerial coordination needed to sustain it, before handing over for a short break and further district-level presentations of field learnings.
