At this stage of life, our adolescent boys and girls need much greater attention to nutrition for their physical and mental development.

Across nearly all of the roughly eighty schools where we are implementing this nutrition curriculum, we have been supporting the Ministry of Education in putting it into practice. Where before there was little engagement, students are now paying more attention and improving. For this we have produced a WASH-and-nutrition diary/journal for adolescent girls in every school, along with a booklet of tasty, nutritious recipes.

[At this point the recording notes a technical glitch.] We want Bangladesh's adolescent boys and girls to grow into their full potential and to be able to contribute to the country as capable citizens.

Nutrition International works with the governments of Bangladesh and several other developing countries, providing various forms of cooperation. We work in five countries in Asia and five countries in Africa, with country offices and staff who work directly with governments on nutrition. We believe international support works best when it strengthens what governments are already doing -- through existing programmes, schemes, and work plans -- rather than running parallel to them, and you will see in what follows how Nutrition International aligns its support with government programmes.

We carry out four main programmes: Vitamin A supplementation; support for government efforts to iodize salt, including whatever technical capacity that requires; work with the Institute of Public Health Nutrition on nutrition programming; and a rice-fortification programme (sometimes referred to as the "food-friendly" programme) that channels fortified rice to poor and marginalised groups through the public food-distribution system. Alongside these four core programmes we also support policy-change initiatives and are involved with several other collaborative efforts.
